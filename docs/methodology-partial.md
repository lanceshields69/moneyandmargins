# How this map works

Last updated {{updated_date}}

This map shows what outside groups reported spending to support or oppose candidates in the 2026 U.S. Senate races. It is a record of filings. It is not a prediction of who will win, and it doesn't show whether any of the spending worked.

**What it counts.** Independent expenditures reported to the FEC: payments by outside groups, such as super PACs, to support or oppose a named candidate without coordinating with the campaign. This version uses the FEC's processed filings. 24- and 48-hour reports are not included, and most recent spending is filed that way, so totals are lower than the full picture, most of all for the last few weeks. It covers the 2026 cycle, including primary season, and places spending in the candidate's state. Supporting and opposing are shown separately and never subtracted.

**What it leaves out.** Donations to groups, candidate campaign spending, party-coordinated spending, where ads actually aired, and filings not yet processed. The latest filing in the data is dated {{latest_filing_date}}.

**Data check in progress.** Totals for some races are being corrected. Each "Spent on ads" share can change as the missing filings are added.

**The forecast.** Chances to win come from VotePredictor's forecast, frozen {{forecast_freeze_date}}. It is one model's estimate. A 55% chance is not a prediction that someone will win.

**Even-handed.** Both parties get the same calculations and labels. When a record is missing, the map says "Incomplete data" and gives the reason.

## What it counts, in detail

Independent expenditures include TV and digital ads, mailers, and field work, not only ads. Outside groups choose whether to label each one as supporting or opposing a candidate when they file. The map uses the FEC's processed periodic reports. A group's 24- and 48-hour reports, which it files within days of spending, are not in these totals. Spending is placed in the candidate's state, which is where the race is, not necessarily where an ad ran.

## What the numbers mean

**Map color.** Total reported spending in the state, support plus oppose, for all candidates. It is total dollars, not per voter.

**Spent on ads.** Each party's share of reported spending that supports its nominee or opposes the other nominee. For independents, the same rule applies to their own race. Because recent spending is missing, the shares may change.

**Predicted to win.** Each nominee's chance of winning in VotePredictor's forecast. It is one model's estimate of a future event.

## What it leaves out, in detail

- 24- and 48-hour reports, which is where most recent spending is filed.
- Donations to groups. The map shows who spent, not who gave them money, and a donation to a group is not the same as money the group spent in a state.
- Candidate campaign spending and party-coordinated spending.
- Electioneering communications, which the FEC tracks separately.
- Spending not tied to a specific candidate, such as an ad against a whole party. {{unattributed_sentence}}

## How it stays even-handed

Both parties get the same calculations, labels, colors, and sort rules. I test this by swapping the parties in the data and checking that every number and label mirrors. When a record is missing or doesn't line up, the map says "Incomplete data" and gives the reason. It doesn't show a zero.

## Sources and updates

Spending: the Federal Election Commission (fec.gov). Forecast: VotePredictor (votepredictor.com). Map boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau. Nominees: compiled from public race pages and checked against FEC records, with state election sites used where a race had a replacement nominee or withdrawal.

The data refreshes twice a day. Reports filed after the last refresh are not included.
