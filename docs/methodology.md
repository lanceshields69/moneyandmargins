# How this map works

Last updated {{updated_date}}

This map shows what outside groups reported spending to support or oppose candidates in the 2026 U.S. Senate races. It is a record of filings. It is not a prediction of who will win, and it doesn't show whether any of the spending worked.

**What it counts.** Independent expenditures reported to the FEC: payments by outside groups, such as super PACs, to support or oppose a named candidate without coordinating with the campaign. That includes the 24- and 48-hour reports and the regular periodic reports, with duplicates removed. It covers the 2026 cycle, including primary season, and places spending in the candidate's state. Supporting and opposing are shown separately and never subtracted.

**What it leaves out.** Donations to groups, candidate campaign spending, party-coordinated spending, where ads actually aired, and filings not yet made. The latest filing in the data is dated {{latest_filing_date}}. Known gaps are listed in the full method.

**The forecast.** Chances to win come from VotePredictor's forecast, frozen {{forecast_freeze_date}}. It is one model's estimate. A 55% chance is not a prediction that someone will win.

**Even-handed.** Both parties get the same calculations and labels. When a record is missing, the map says "Incomplete data" and gives the reason.

## What it counts, in detail

Independent expenditures include TV and digital ads, mailers, and field work, not only ads. Outside groups choose whether to label each one as supporting or opposing a candidate when they file. The map uses the FEC's itemized filings. Where a group has filed a 24- or 48-hour report and later a periodic report covering the same dates, the map uses only the periodic report for those dates, and amended or terminated rows are removed, so each expenditure is counted once. Spending is placed in the candidate's state, which is where the race is, not necessarily where an ad ran.

## What the numbers mean

**Map color.** Total reported spending in the state, support plus oppose, for all candidates. It is total dollars, not per voter.

**Spent on ads.** Each party's share of reported spending that supports its nominee or opposes the other nominee. For independents, the same rule applies to their own race.

**National card.** The same calculation across the 35 races. It skips races with incomplete data and says how many.

**Predicted to win.** Each nominee's chance of winning in VotePredictor's forecast. It is one model's estimate of a future event.

## What it leaves out, in detail

- Donations to groups. The map shows who spent, not who gave them money, and a donation to a group is not the same as money the group spent in a state.
- Candidate campaign spending and party-coordinated spending.
- Electioneering communications, which the FEC tracks separately.
- Spending not tied to a specific candidate, such as an ad against a whole party. {{unattributed_sentence}}

## Known gaps

I'm still working on these, and I'll note here when each one is fixed.

- **Late filings.** Groups file new reports every day through Election Day, and the FEC takes time to process them. Anything filed after the last refresh is missing.
- **Duplicate removal is imperfect for a few groups.** For {{approx_committee_count}} committees, the FEC data doesn't show which dates their regular reports cover, so I match their notices to those reports by amount, date, and payee. That can occasionally leave a duplicate in or remove a real row. Those rows total {{approx_total}}.
- **Primary-season spending.** {{primary_sentence}}
- **Contrast ads.** A contrast ad can be reported on two lines, so a state's total can count some spending twice.
- **Spending not tied to a candidate.** {{unattributed_sentence}}

## How it stays even-handed

Both parties get the same calculations, labels, colors, and sort rules. I test this by swapping the parties in the data and checking that every number and label mirrors. When a record is missing or doesn't line up, the map says "Incomplete data" and gives the reason. It doesn't show a zero.

## Sources and updates

Spending: the Federal Election Commission (fec.gov). Forecast: VotePredictor (votepredictor.com). Map boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau. Nominees: compiled from public race pages and checked against FEC records, with state election sites used where a race had a replacement nominee or withdrawal.

The data refreshes every few hours through Election Day. Reports filed after the last refresh are not included.
